Dennis Verhoeven

Assistant Professor, Department of Knowledge, Technology and Organization (KTO), SKEMA Business School

I work on the economics of innovation and science.

I'm an assistant professor at the department of Knowledge, Technology, and Organization (KTO) at SKEMA Business School. I'm also a research associate of the Centre for Economic Performance at LSE and a research fellow at the department of Management, Strategy, and Innovation at KU Leuven, where I obtained my PhD in Applied Economics.

Current work focuses on industrial policy, air pollution, knowledge diversity in teams, and the gender productivity gap in science. Earlier projects focus on radical innovation and the measurement of technological novelty.

Selected work

Air Pollution and Innovation

with Felix Bracht · Journal of Environmental Economics and Management, 2025

A drop in fine particulate matter equal to Europe's average yearly decline raises patented inventions in a region by 1.2%, which lifts the estimated economic cost of air pollution by about three quarters.

Efficient Industrial Policy for Innovation: Standing on the Shoulders of Hidden Giants

with Charlotte Guillard, Ralf Martin, Pierre Mohnen and Catherine Thomas · under review

Targeting R&D subsidies at fields with the highest spillovers raises within-country returns by at least 40% over uniform subsidies; coordinating across the OECD adds another 30%.

Knowledge Spillovers from Clean Innovation: A Tradeoff between Growth and Climate?

with Ralf Martin · R&R at Nature Climate Change

Supporting clean innovation is a win-win rather than a tradeoff, but most of the return leaks across borders — which is an argument for supranational coordination.

Do Broader Teams Lead to More Innovation?

Rej&R at Management Science

Using inventor deaths as quasi-random variation in team breadth, I find no effect on innovation — the usual positive relationship does not survive controlling for who sorts into broad teams.
