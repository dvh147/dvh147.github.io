Research Notes

Research Notes is a growing collection of short literature reviews on the economics of innovation, each arguing a single narrow claim from cited papers. This is a little side project where I let a research agent find, read, and analyze research papers on a given topic. As this is heavily AI-reliant, I generally do not trust the output blindly, but I do like reading the posts for inspiration.

Dirty air makes fewer inventions, and that raises its price by three quarters

2 September 2026

The standard bill for air pollution is measured on mortality and same-year GDP. Neither can see the inventions that never happen, and those turn out to be worth about as much again.

Patent citations cannot tell prize-winning science from ordinary science

2 September 2026

Nearly all prize-winning papers land in the top half of Science Rank. On the usual measure, direct patent-to-paper citations, they land about where a coin flip would put them.

Over half of the world's knowledge spillovers are invisible to citation counts

2 September 2026

Counting forward citations misses inventions whose influence runs through long chains of follow-on work. Those Hidden Giants carry 54% of all global spillover value, and knowing where they are changes what R&D policy should fund.

Clean innovation is a win-win, and most of the winnings leave the country

2 September 2026

Subsidising clean R&D beats an untargeted scheme on knowledge spillovers alone, before you count any climate benefit. But only about 23% of the value stays in the country that paid, which is why the US case for it disappears when you look only at national returns.

Broader teams invent better things. Broadening teams does not.

2 September 2026

The link between team breadth and invention quality is one of the more robust regularities in the field, and it does not survive being asked a causal question.

Putting an inventor in an unfamiliar field costs a firm 25% more

2 September 2026

Inventors who switch technological fields produce the most valuable inventions, and yet switching keeps getting rarer. Allocation frictions inside firms reconcile the two, and they are large enough that easing them would raise idea generation by half.

Firms with the most market power make the most valuable inventions, for everyone

2 September 2026

A standard deviation more markup goes with innovation worth 12% more, and the knowledge spillovers rise alongside the private value. Conditioning on private value, though, high-markup firms are not unusually generous to others.

The clean innovation slump looks partly like a loss of nerve

2 September 2026

Clean patenting has fallen since around 2010. The spread of private values narrowed at the same time, which is what a retreat from risky R&D looks like, and clean inventions are the risky ones.

Novel inventions get fewer citations on average, and that is the point

2 September 2026

If you define radical inventions by their eventual impact, you only ever study the ones that worked. Measured before the fact instead, novelty predicts variance rather than a higher average, which is what a high-risk profile looks like.

Four words for exceptional innovation, and the field cannot tell them apart

2 September 2026

Radical, breakthrough, discontinuous and disruptive are used by papers with the same intellectual foundations and the same topics. The root cause is treating novelty and impact as the same thing, which hides the innovations that are one without the other.

How many citations a patent has depends on who is counting

2 September 2026

Three routine choices in building a patent citation count produce measures that differ substantially, and pick out different patents as the highly cited ones. Correcting for patent families with the broader INPADOC definition is the most stable option.
